An introduction to the data of :
Horse Racing

Introduction

For new and casual fans, learning about horse racing may seem daunting. This website aims to introduce the history and records of the sport through visualizations. They are organized into three main sections: general (for broad data), local race courses (for specific data regarding particular racecourses), and lineage (for detailing the legacies of significant horses).
Note: This dataset is incomplete and is greatly focused on Great Britain races, so be aware that the data is not fully accurate.

General Visualizations

Note: To address the Great Britain (GB)-skewed dataset, there are buttons to filter and view specific data, either GB-focused or non-GB data.
Races Per Year
Total number of races held each year from 1990 to 2020.
Note: The suspicious shift in races around 2002-2003 is likely a data error.
Top 10 Horses By Wins
Horses with the most first‑place finishes.
Top 10 Jockeys By Wins
Jockeys with the highest number of winning rides.
Note: Although this dataset draws from 1990-2020 data, which encapsulates AP McCoy's jockey career, it only recorded 737 of his over 4000 career wins. This exemplifies the inaccuracy of the dataset.
Profits From Win Bets (CAD)
These are the average betting earnings for each horse if you placed a £1 bet on them to win every race. Most values are negative because horse race betting usually loses money overall.
Note: The dataset was converted from pound sterling to CAD (x1.85).
Total Prize Money From Wins (CAD)
This shows how much money each horse actually earned from winning races, converted to CAD.
Top Racecourses By Average Prize (CAD)
This calculates total prize amount over number of races, and filters out racecourses with less than 50 races in the data to prevent outliers.
Total Races By Country
This represents how skewed the dataset focus is for Great Britain and is why there are filters for this general section.
Fastest Winning Time Per Year (seconds)
The fastest recorded winning time each year.
Note: Due to small variations in records, the Y axis ranges from 99 to 101 seconds.
